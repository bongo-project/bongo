| B13 | **(research PRD) Backup that scales past a few GB.** Operator raised 2026-08-22: the current backup does not survive hundreds/thousands of accounts. Correct, and it is THREE problems. (1) **Duplication** — `backup-extras.sh` tars everything nightly, but blobs are content-addressed and immutable, so it re-copies all historical mail to capture one day of new mail. (2) **Downtime stops being free** — measured 2026-08-22: 11.6s stop->start, entirely graceful shutdown, because tar of ~26MB is instant. gzip runs ~50MB/s, so past roughly 1GB the window becomes tar-bound and grows linearly; at 100GB it is an hour of nightly downtime. (3) **Restore time** — RTO becomes hours. **The fix the architecture already supports:** split the halves. Blobs are immutable -> incremental/dedup backup (restic/borg), nightly cost proportional to NEW mail. Pebble is small and mutable and supports CHECKPOINTS — a consistent snapshot while running, which bongo already uses in `FSM.Snapshot`. Together that removes the container stop entirely. **Ordering is load-bearing: snapshot Pebble FIRST, then copy blobs.** Delivery writes the blob before recording metadata, so every blob referenced by the snapshot already exists; the reverse order can capture metadata pointing at an uncopied blob. Also worth covering in the PRD: interaction with blob GC (currently `dryrun`), whether per-user restore is wanted at that scale (tar-of-everything cannot do it), and verifying the new scheme with `verify-restore.sh`. Supersedes the stop-tar-start approach in [[zero-downtime-backup-plan]]. | TODO | Raised 2026-08-22; not urgent at current volume, becomes urgent as accounts grow |
